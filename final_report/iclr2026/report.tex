\begin{abstract}
World models such as DreamerV3 learn a sequential generative latent by training
a recurrent latent-dynamics model on streams of observations. Unsupervised
representation learning, by contrast, is usually studied on static data. We ask
what the sequential latent encodes that a static, per-frame model does not. We
freeze a DreamerV3 recurrent state-space model (RSSM) and a static frame-level
variational autoencoder (VAE) trained on the same DeepMind Control
proprioceptive Walker-Walk observation stream, then probe both with held-out
ridge and MLP regression across three axes: the probe target (current or future
simulator state, and derived dynamical quantities), the probe site (encoder,
RSSM posterior, RSSM prior, VAE latent), and the temporal receptive field given
to the static model. We find that (i) per-frame current state is decoded
comparably well by both models, and within the RSSM this content is carried
primarily by the deterministic state rather than the categorical stochastic
latent; (ii) the static VAE's per-frame latents, aggregated post-hoc over the
past, match and slightly exceed the RSSM at current-state decoding, whereas a few
recent frames do not; (iii) the RSSM carries far more future-state information in
its action-conditioned prior than in a linear readout of its posterior
($R^2\approx0.50$ versus $0.16$ at a 20-step horizon), and it makes gait phase
linearly decodable; and (iv) generative likelihood and probe \rtwo{} decouple:
the RSSM reconstructs far better than the VAE without a matching gain in linearly
decodable state.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A world model is a learned representation of states that can be rolled forward
to predict future states \citep{ha2018worldmodels}. DreamerV3
\citep{hafner2025dreamerv3} instantiates this with a recurrent state-space model
(RSSM) \citep{hafner2019planet}: an observation is encoded into a stochastic
latent that is concatenated with a deterministic recurrent state, and the model
is trained to reconstruct observations and to predict its own next latent. The
resulting representation is sequential, shaped by temporal context and a learned
transition model. Most unsupervised representation learning, by contrast, is
developed and analyzed on static data, for example a VAE \citep{kingma2014vae}
over images. Because world models are increasingly reused as pretrained
representations for downstream control, what their sequential latent captures,
relative to a static encoder of the same inputs, bears on when sequential
pretraining is worthwhile. This motivates a concrete question: what does a
sequential generative latent encode beyond what a static, per-frame model
extracts from the same observations?

We answer this question with a controlled probing comparison. We freeze a trained
DreamerV3 RSSM and a static frame-level VAE that uses the same encoder and decoder
architecture as DreamerV3 (trained from scratch, not shared weights), the same
observation model, and the same training frames, so that architecture and data
are held fixed and sequential structure is the principal difference. We then probe
both with held-out
regression \citep{alain2016probes}, varying the probe target, the probe site
(including the RSSM prior and open-loop imagination), and the temporal receptive
field given to the static model, and we report held-out generative likelihood
alongside. Section~\ref{sec:results} previews the findings: per-frame state
content is shared between the two models; a post-hoc temporal aggregation of the
static latents closes the current-state gap; the RSSM carries far more
future-state information in its prior than in a posterior readout, and makes gait
phase linearly decodable; and generative likelihood tracks something different
from probe accuracy. Code and figures are released for
reproducibility.\footnote{Code: \texttt{probing/} in the project repository;
figures are regenerated by \texttt{result\_images/build\_summary\_figures.py}.}

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Probing representations.} Linear and shallow probes are a standard
tool for asking what a frozen representation encodes, by measuring how well a
simple readout recovers a target attribute \citep{alain2016probes}. A probe
score depends on the probe family and the target: it lower-bounds decodable
information rather than measuring it, a linear and a nonlinear probe can rank
representations differently, and a pooled $R^2$ over a vector target is
variance-weighted. We therefore fix the probe family across all comparisons,
report a linear probe as primary with a nonlinear one as a robustness check, and
flag where the two disagree.

\paragraph{World-model representations.} World models learn representations by
predicting future observations from latent state
\citep{ha2018worldmodels,hafner2019planet,hafner2020dreamer,hafner2025dreamerv3},
and are increasingly used to pretrain representations for downstream control
without task reward \citep{sekar2020plan2explore,liu2021aps,laskin2021urlb}. That
setting makes the content of the learned latent the object of interest, yet such
work usually reports downstream return rather than what the latent itself
captures. We instead probe the frozen latent directly and, to isolate the effect
of sequential structure, compare it against a static VAE
\citep{kingma2014vae,higgins2017betavae} that shares the encoder and decoder
architecture and the training data.

\section{Background and setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{RSSM world model.} At each step $t$ the RSSM maintains a
deterministic recurrent state $h_t=f_\phi(h_{t-1},z_{t-1},a_{t-1})$ and a
stochastic latent with a posterior $z_t\sim q_\phi(z_t\mid h_t,x_t)$ that sees
the observation $x_t$ and a prior $\hat z_t\sim p_\phi(\hat z_t\mid h_t)$ that
does not. The model state is $s_t=(h_t,z_t)$ and a decoder reconstructs
$\hat x_t=d_\phi(s_t)$. In our configuration $z_t$ is a set of $32$ categorical
variables with $4$ classes each ($128$ dimensions), and $h_t$ has $512$
dimensions. The model is trained with reconstruction and a KL term that aligns
posterior and prior \citep{hafner2025dreamerv3}.

\paragraph{Static VAE.} Our baseline removes the recurrence and dynamics while
holding everything else fixed. It uses an encoder with the same architecture as
DreamerV3's, trained from scratch, to map a single frame $x_t$ to a
diagonal-Gaussian latent $q(z\mid x_t)=\mathcal{N}(\mu(x_t),
\mathrm{diag}\,\sigma^2(x_t))$, and a decoder matched to the RSSM's vector
decoder. It is trained on the per-frame objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\beta(x) = \underbrace{\E_{q(z\mid x)}\!\big[-\log p(x\mid z)\big]}_{\text{reconstruction}}
\;+\; \beta\,\KL\!\big(q(z\mid x)\,\Vert\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\big),
\label{eq:elbo}
\end{equation}
the (negative) evidence lower bound for $\beta{=}1$ \citep{kingma2014vae,
higgins2017betavae}, optimized with the reparameterization $z=\mu+\sigma\odot
\epsilon$. Both models use the same observation model, a unit-variance Gaussian
in $\mathrm{symlog}$ space, $\mathrm{symlog}(x)=\mathrm{sign}(x)\log(1+|x|)$, so
that $-\log p(x\mid z)=\tfrac12\Vert\mathrm{symlog}(x)-d(z)\Vert^2+\text{const}$
and held-out log-likelihoods are computed identically. We set the VAE latent to
$128$ dimensions to match the RSSM's stochastic latent. Because the encoder and
decoder architectures, observation model, and training frames are held fixed, a
probe gap reflects sequential structure and the change of latent variable rather
than the encoder or decoder; the latent family, objective, and dimensionality
also differ, which we revisit in the limitations.

\paragraph{Task, models, and data.} We use DeepMind Control
\citep{tassa2018dmc} Walker-Walk with proprioceptive observations
(\texttt{orientations}, \texttt{height}, \texttt{velocity}). We train three
DreamerV3 seeds and one VAE per seed on that seed's replay buffer; two seeds run
to $500$K environment steps and one to $1.1$M. Probe figures show all three
seeds as points with the bar or line at their mean. The $1.1$M seed is usually
highest, but by no more than the gap between the two $500$K seeds, so we do not
separate it in the probe figures; the training-length effect instead appears in
generative likelihood, which we report per seed (\S\ref{sec:gen}). Probe
trajectories are $40$ held-out episodes ($1000$ steps) rolled out from the frozen
evaluation policy, logging the full MuJoCo physics state
($q_{\text{pos}}\,|\,q_{\text{vel}}$), which the replay buffer does not store.

\section{Probing methodology}
\label{sec:method}

For a frozen representation $z$ and a target $y$ we fit a probe $g$ and report
the held-out coefficient of determination
\begin{equation}
R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y-g(z))^2}{\sum (y-\bar y_{\text{train}})^2},
\end{equation}
against the train-set mean baseline, so an uninformative representation scores
$\approx 0$ and negative values are possible on held-out episodes. For vector
targets we report the pooled $R^2$ over all dimensions, a variance-weighted
average that counts high-variance components such as joint velocities more. The probe
family is fixed across all sites and targets, namely a closed-form ridge
regressor (regularization chosen on a validation split of the training episodes)
and a small two-hidden-layer MLP, so differences reflect the representation
rather than the probe. We report the ridge (linear) probe in the main text and
treat the MLP as a robustness check, with its scores included in the released
CSV; the conclusions below hold under both probes except where noted. Train and
test splits are by episode. We vary three axes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Probe target:} the current simulator state; the future state at
horizon $k\in\{1,5,20\}$; and derived dynamical quantities (gait phase, encoded
as $(\cos,\sin)$ of the analytic-signal phase of the antiphase hip oscillation;
discounted return-to-go; time-to-fall).
\item \textbf{Probe site:} encoder output; RSSM posterior ($h_t$ and $\mathbb{E}[z_t]$,
also probed separately); RSSM prior, both one-step and open-loop $k$-step
(roll the prior forward with the recorded actions and probe the imagined state);
VAE encoder output; VAE latent mean.
\item \textbf{Receptive field:} the VAE latent with a growing causal temporal
window. For $K\in\{1,4,16\}$ we concatenate the most recent $K$ latents; for
$K{=}\text{all}$ we concatenate the current latent with the causal mean of all
latents up to that step. This measures how much sequential content a static
model recovers by post-hoc temporal aggregation.
\end{itemize}
We additionally report held-out reconstruction and one-step predictive
log-likelihood, computed identically for both models (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

\subsection{Per-frame current state is shared; within the RSSM it lies mainly in $h_t$}
Figure~\ref{fig:probes} (left) shows that the encoder, RSSM posterior, RSSM
deterministic state, VAE encoder, and VAE latent all decode the current state at
$R^2\approx0.69$ to $0.74$. Within the RSSM, this content is carried mainly by
the deterministic state ($R^2\approx0.70$); the categorical stochastic latent
alone is weaker but not negligible ($\approx0.40$ under the linear probe,
$\approx0.55$ under the MLP). The deterministic state on its own
($0.70$) is about level with the VAE latent ($0.69$); the full posterior's small
advantage ($0.74$) comes from combining it with complementary information in the
stochastic latent. At equal width ($128$ dimensions), the continuous VAE latent
($0.69$) is a much stronger linear state code than the RSSM's categorical latent
($0.40$), so the edge does not come from the stochastic latent on its own. A
nonlinear MLP probe does not favor the RSSM here either, consistent with
per-frame content being shared.

\subsection{Predictive content: open-loop prior versus posterior readout}
\label{sec:pred}
A linear readout of future state from the posterior decays sharply with horizon
($R^2 = 0.74,\,0.50,\,0.30,\,0.16$ at $k=0,1,5,20$; Figure~\ref{fig:probes},
right). Rolling the prior forward with the recorded actions and probing the
imagined state retains much more information ($R^2 = 0.72,\,0.66,\,0.50$ at
$k=1,5,20$). At a $20$-step horizon the action-conditioned prior reaches
$\approx0.50$ versus $0.16$ for a linear readout of the same future state from
the posterior, roughly three times the future-state content. This advantage is
also robust to the probe: under the MLP the prior still reaches $R^2\approx0.43$
at $20$ steps while the posterior readout falls to about zero. The RSSM's learned
dynamics, not a readout of the current latent, carry the future. This compares
two read-outs of the RSSM; we do not equip the VAE with a learned transition
model, so the result locates the RSSM's predictive content rather than claiming
it forecasts better than a static representation with its own dynamics. Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:openloop} visualizes a single open-loop rollout and the
prediction error growing with horizon.

\subsection{Post-hoc temporal aggregation of static features closes the current-state gap}
Figure~\ref{fig:gap} (left) shows the VAE latent's current-state \rtwo{} rising
from $0.69$ (one frame) through $0.70$ and $0.71$ (the most recent $4$ and $16$
frames) to $0.78$ once it also includes the causal mean over the whole episode,
at which point it overtakes the RSSM posterior ($0.74$). Concatenating a handful
of recent frames is not enough; only whole-episode aggregation closes and
reverses the gap. This aggregation is a hand-specified causal summary applied on
top of the per-frame latents, not learned recurrence; the point is that the
current-state content the RSSM exposes is recoverable from static features by
simple temporal pooling, so the sequential representation's value is not in
richer current-state encoding.

\subsection{Gait phase: the RSSM makes a cyclic quantity linearly decodable}
Figure~\ref{fig:gap} (right) shows the one target where the RSSM clearly leads
under a linear probe: gait phase is decoded at $R^2\approx0.95$ from the
posterior versus $\approx0.74$ from the VAE latent ($+0.21$, consistent across
all three seeds). Phase is about position within a cycle, which a single frame
cannot disambiguate from a linear readout. This advantage is largely one of
linear accessibility, however: a nonlinear MLP probe recovers most of the gait
phase from the static latent as well ($0.89$ versus $0.95$), so the RSSM mainly
reorganizes this cyclic information to be linearly readable rather than encoding
something the VAE lacks. (Return-to-go is near-zero for both models, and
time-to-fall is unreliable due to censoring, since a competent walker rarely
falls; see Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}.)

\subsection{Generative quality and probe quality decouple}
\label{sec:gen}
Table~\ref{tab:nll} reports held-out negative log-likelihood. At matched
training, the RSSM reconstructs the current frame far better than the VAE ($0.62$
to $0.67$ versus $1.77$ to $1.95$ NLL for the two $500$K seeds, and better on
$90$ to $97\%$ of frames), yet its current-state probe \rtwo{} is only marginally
higher (Figure~\ref{fig:probes}, left). Better reconstruction therefore does not
translate into proportionally more linearly decodable state. The longer-trained
seed~0 points the same way, with much lower NLL ($0.36$) but probe \rtwo{} within
seed noise of the others; with a single $1.1$M run this is suggestive of a
training-length effect rather than a controlled one. The two metrics agree only
partially, a caution against using either alone as a proxy for representation
quality.

\section{Discussion}

Across a comparison that holds the encoder, decoder, observation model, and data
fixed, the sequential generative latent does not encode more about the current
observable state than a static VAE; a hand-specified causal summary of the static
model's past latents matches it. Within the RSSM, the future-state information
lives in the action-conditioned prior, which forecasts $20$ steps ahead far
better than a linear readout of the posterior and is robust to the probe family;
we read this as locating the RSSM's predictive content, not as a claim that it
forecasts better than a static representation equipped with its own dynamics. The
RSSM also makes gait phase linearly decodable, though a nonlinear probe recovers
most of that from the static latent, so this is an advantage of linear
accessibility rather than of information the static model lacks. Separately, the
RSSM reconstructs far better than the VAE without a matching gain in what a
linear probe reads off, so reconstruction likelihood and probe \rtwo{} should not
be treated as interchangeable measures of representation quality.

\textbf{Limitations.} Holding architecture and data fixed still leaves several
differences between the models: the VAE latent is continuous rather than
categorical, its prior and objective differ, and the probed posterior feature is
higher-dimensional ($640$ versus $128$). We report matched-width stochastic- and
deterministic-state probes to control for dimensionality, but the other
differences remain, so we treat sequential structure as the principal rather than
the sole factor behind any gap. The predictive result (\S\ref{sec:pred}) compares
two read-outs of the RSSM; we do not train an action-conditioned dynamics model
on VAE features, so it locates the RSSM's predictive content rather than showing
superiority over a static representation with its own dynamics. We report a
pooled, variance-weighted $R^2$ over state dimensions, which counts high-variance
components such as velocities more; per-component probing is left to future work.
We study one domain (Walker), one modality (proprioception, where a single frame
nearly determines the current state, which may inflate the shared per-frame
content), and one model size. Probe \rtwo{} lower-bounds decodable information
under a fixed probe family and is not mutual information. Finally, only one seed
ran to $1.1$M steps, so the training-length effect on generative likelihood is
suggestive rather than controlled.

\section{Null and unreliable targets}
\label{app:nulls}
Two derived targets are reported for completeness but excluded from the main
findings. Return-to-go (discounted future reward) has near-zero \rtwo{} for both
models ($\approx-0.01$ posterior, $\approx0$ VAE) on Walker-Walk, where per-step
reward is nearly constant and not linearly decodable. Time-to-fall is highly
seed-dependent (posterior \rtwo{} of $0.06$, $0.50$, and $0.98$ across seeds)
because a competent walker rarely falls, so the target is dominated by rare,
heavily right-censored events; we therefore do not draw conclusions from it.

\section{Reproducibility details}
DreamerV3 uses the \texttt{dmc\_proprio}/\texttt{size1m} configuration
($h_t\in\mathbb{R}^{512}$, $z_t$ a set of $32{\times}4$ categoricals,
encoder and decoder MLPs of width $64$, depth $3$, symlog preprocessing). The
VAE uses the same encoder and decoder architecture, with a $128$-dimensional Gaussian
latent, $\beta{=}1$, trained from scratch for $60$K steps (Adam, lr
$3\times10^{-4}$) on each seed's replay buffer. Probes use ridge (regularization selected on a validation
split) and a two-hidden-layer, width-$256$ ReLU MLP. The probe set is $40$ held-out
episodes of $1000$ steps from the frozen evaluation policy with logged physics
state. All figures are regenerated by
\texttt{result\_images/build\_summary\_figures.py} from the per-seed outputs.
