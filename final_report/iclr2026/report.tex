\begin{abstract}
World models such as DreamerV3 learn sequential generative latents from streams
of observations, while unsupervised representation learning is often studied on
static data. We ask what this sequential latent encodes beyond a static,
per-frame model. We freeze a DreamerV3 recurrent state-space model (RSSM) and a
static frame-level VAE trained on the same proprioceptive Walker-Walk stream,
then probe both with held-out ridge and MLP regressors across targets (current
state, future state, gait phase), probe sites, and the static model's temporal
receptive field. In this near-fully observed proprioceptive setting, per-frame
current state is decoded comparably well by both models; static VAE latents
aggregated post-hoc over the past match and slightly exceed the RSSM at
current-state decoding, while a few recent frames do not. These current-state
results do not cover pixel or partially observed settings. For prediction, the
RSSM prior reaches $R^2\approx0.50$ at a 20-step horizon, versus $0.16$ for a
posterior readout and $0.46$ for learned dynamics in the VAE latent space; under
the primary linear probe, the RSSM leads at every horizon, though the margin
narrows. The recurrent posterior also makes gait phase linearly decodable.
Finally, generative likelihood and probe \rtwo{} decouple: the RSSM has lower
reconstruction NLL than the VAE without a matching gain in linearly decodable
state.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A world model is a learned state representation that can be rolled forward to
predict future states \citep{ha2018worldmodels}. DreamerV3
\citep{hafner2025dreamerv3} uses an RSSM \citep{hafner2019planet}: observations
enter a stochastic latent concatenated with a deterministic recurrent state, and
the model reconstructs observations while predicting its next latent. Because
world models are increasingly reused as pretrained representations for control,
we ask what this sequential generative latent encodes beyond a static,
per-frame encoder trained on the same observations.

We freeze a trained DreamerV3 RSSM and a static VAE with the same encoder and
decoder architecture (trained from scratch, not shared weights), observation
model, and training frames, so architecture and data are held fixed. We probe
both with held-out regression \citep{alain2016probes}, varying target, probe
site, and the static model's temporal receptive field, and report held-out
generative likelihood. In proprioceptive Walker-Walk, per-frame state content is
shared, post-hoc aggregation closes the current-state gap, the RSSM prior
retains higher future-state \rtwo{} than a posterior readout and makes gait phase
linearly decodable, and lower generative NLL does not imply a richer linear state
code. Code and figures are released for reproducibility.\footnote{Code:
\texttt{probing/} in the project repository; figures are regenerated by
\texttt{result\_images/build\_summary\_figures.py}.}

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Probing representations.} Linear and shallow probes measure how well a
frozen representation supports a simple readout of a target attribute
\citep{alain2016probes}. Because probe scores lower-bound decodable information,
depend on the probe family, and are variance-weighted for vector targets, we fix
the probe family, use ridge as primary and an MLP as a robustness check, and flag
disagreements. Closest in spirit, ST-DIM introduced an Atari benchmark that
evaluates unsupervised representations by recovery of ground-truth state
variables \citep{anand2019atari}; we use the same diagnostic logic for a
narrower controlled RSSM-vs-VAE comparison.

\paragraph{World-model representations.} World models learn latent states by
predicting future observations
\citep{ha2018worldmodels,hafner2019planet,hafner2020dreamer,hafner2025dreamerv3};
related objectives such as CPC also use future prediction as a representation
signal \citep{oord2018cpc}. Reward-free world-model pretraining usually reports
downstream return \citep{sekar2020plan2explore,liu2021aps,laskin2021urlb}; we
instead probe the frozen latent directly and compare it with a static VAE
\citep{kingma2014vae,higgins2017betavae} sharing architecture and data.

\section{Background and setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{RSSM world model.} At each step, the RSSM keeps a deterministic state
$h_t=f_\phi(h_{t-1},z_{t-1},a_{t-1})$, a posterior
$z_t\sim q_\phi(z_t\mid h_t,x_t)$ that sees the observation, and a prior
$\hat z_t\sim p_\phi(\hat z_t\mid h_t)$ that does not. A decoder reconstructs
$\hat x_t=d_\phi(h_t,z_t)$. Our $z_t$ has $32$ categorical variables with $4$
classes each ($128$ dimensions), and $h_t$ has $512$ dimensions. Training uses
reconstruction and a KL term aligning posterior and prior
\citep{hafner2025dreamerv3}.

\paragraph{Static VAE.} The baseline removes recurrence and dynamics while
holding encoder/decoder architecture, observation model, and training frames
fixed. It maps a single frame to a diagonal-Gaussian latent and is trained on
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\beta(x) = \underbrace{\E_{q(z\mid x)}\!\big[-\log p(x\mid z)\big]}_{\text{reconstruction}}
\;+\; \beta\,\KL\!\big(q(z\mid x)\,\Vert\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\big),
\label{eq:elbo}
\end{equation}
the negative ELBO for $\beta{=}1$ \citep{kingma2014vae,higgins2017betavae}. Both
models use the same unit-variance Gaussian observation model in symlog space, so
held-out log-likelihoods are computed identically. The VAE latent has $128$
dimensions to match the RSSM stochastic latent. A probe gap therefore reflects
sequential structure and latent-family differences rather than the encoder,
decoder, or data; remaining differences are revisited in the limitations.

\paragraph{Task, models, and data.} We use DeepMind Control Walker-Walk
\citep{tassa2018dmc} with proprioceptive observations. We train three DreamerV3
seeds and one VAE per seed on that seed's replay buffer; two seeds run to $500$K
environment steps and one to $1.1$M. Probe figures show all seeds as points with
the mean as bar or line. The $1.1$M seed does not systematically dominate the
probe scores, so we show it with the others; a possible training-length effect
instead appears in generative likelihood, reported per seed (\S\ref{sec:gen}).
Probe trajectories are $40$ held-out episodes of
$1000$ steps from the frozen evaluation policy, with full MuJoCo physics state
logged for targets.

\section{Probing methodology}
\label{sec:method}

For a frozen representation $z$ and a target $y$ we fit a probe $g$ and report
the held-out coefficient of determination
\begin{equation}
R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y-g(z))^2}{\sum (y-\bar y_{\text{train}})^2},
\end{equation}
against the train-set mean baseline; uninformative representations score
$\approx 0$ and held-out scores can be negative. For vector targets we report
pooled, variance-weighted $R^2$. We use the same ridge regressor and
two-hidden-layer MLP at all sites, report ridge in the main text, and treat MLP
as a robustness check; conclusions hold under both probes except where noted.
Train and test splits are by episode. We vary:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Probe target:} current state; future state at
$k\in\{1,5,20\}$; gait phase (encoded as $(\cos,\sin)$ of the analytic-signal
phase of the antiphase hip oscillation); discounted return-to-go; and
time-to-fall.
\item \textbf{Probe site:} encoder output; RSSM posterior ($h_t$ and $\mathbb{E}[z_t]$,
also probed separately); RSSM prior, both one-step and open-loop $k$-step
(roll the prior forward with the recorded actions and probe the imagined state);
VAE encoder output; VAE latent mean; and the VAE latent rolled open-loop by a
learned action-conditioned dynamics model (\S\ref{sec:pred}), the static analog
of the RSSM prior.
\item \textbf{Receptive field:} the VAE latent with a causal window. For
$K\in\{1,4,16\}$ we concatenate recent latents; for $K{=}\text{all}$ we use the
current latent plus the causal mean over the episode.
\end{itemize}
We additionally report held-out reconstruction and one-step predictive
log-likelihood, computed identically for both models (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

\subsection{Per-frame current state is shared; within the RSSM it lies mainly in $h_t$}
Figure~\ref{fig:probes} (left) shows that the encoder, RSSM posterior, RSSM
deterministic state, VAE encoder, and VAE latent all decode current state at
$R^2\approx0.69$--$0.74$. This content sits mainly in the RSSM deterministic
state ($\approx0.70$); the categorical stochastic latent alone is weaker
($\approx0.40$ linear, $\approx0.55$ MLP). The deterministic state alone
($0.70$) is about level with the VAE latent ($0.69$); the posterior's small
advantage ($0.74$) comes from combining it with complementary information in the
stochastic latent. At equal width, the continuous VAE
latent is a stronger linear state code than the RSSM stochastic latent
($0.69$ versus $0.40$). The MLP does not favor the RSSM either, consistent with
per-frame content being shared.

\subsection{Predictive content: open-loop prior versus posterior readout}
\label{sec:pred}
A linear readout of future state from the posterior decays sharply
($R^2 = 0.74,\,0.50,\,0.30,\,0.16$ at $k=0,1,5,20$; Figure~\ref{fig:probes},
right). Rolling the prior forward with recorded actions retains higher \rtwo{}
($0.72,\,0.66,\,0.50$ at $k=1,5,20$); at $20$ steps this is about three times
the posterior-readout \rtwo{} ($0.50$ versus $0.16$), and the MLP shows the same
prior-vs-posterior pattern ($0.43$ versus about zero). To test whether this is
only the benefit of having dynamics, we fit a post-hoc VAE-latent forward model
$g(z_t,a_t)\approx z_{t+1}-z_t$ on probe train episodes and roll it open-loop.
This baseline is asymmetric by design: the RSSM transition was co-trained with
its latent, whereas VAE dynamics are fit after representation learning on frozen
latents. Even so, the baseline recovers much more future state than a static
latent readout ($0.53,\,0.49,\,0.46$ versus $0.32,\,0.19,\,0.11$). Under the
primary linear probe, the RSSM prior still leads within every seed
($0.72,\,0.66,\,0.50$ versus $0.53,\,0.49,\,0.46$): the unrounded mean margin
shrinks from $+0.18$ at $k=1$ (seed range $+0.15$ to $+0.22$) to $+0.05$ at
$k=20$ (range $+0.02$ to $+0.07$). Under the MLP, the RSSM and VAE-dynamics
models are separated at $k=1$ and $k=5$ but essentially tied at $k=20$, so the
head-to-head advantage is strongest in the linear-probe setting. Both
action-conditioned models approach similar long-horizon accuracy as open-loop
error compounds (Appendix~\ref{app:openloop}, Figure~\ref{fig:openloop}); the
RSSM advantage is sharpest at short horizons, where its co-trained transition is
most accurate.

\subsection{Post-hoc temporal aggregation of static features closes the current-state gap}
Figure~\ref{fig:gap} (left) shows VAE current-state \rtwo{} rising from $0.69$
(one frame) through $0.70$ and $0.71$ (the most recent $4$ and $16$ frames) to
$0.78$ with the causal whole-episode mean, overtaking the RSSM posterior
($0.74$). Concatenating a handful of recent frames is not enough; only
whole-episode aggregation closes and reverses the gap. Thus the
current-state content the RSSM exposes is recoverable from static features by a
hand-specified temporal summary, not learned recurrence.

\subsection{Gait phase: the RSSM makes a cyclic quantity linearly decodable}
Figure~\ref{fig:gap} (right) shows the largest linear RSSM--VAE gap: gait phase
is decoded at $R^2\approx0.95$ from the posterior versus $\approx0.74$ from the
VAE latent ($+0.21$; seed ranges $0.93$--$0.96$ versus $0.72$--$0.77$). Phase
is position within a cycle, which a single frame cannot disambiguate from a
linear readout. A nonlinear MLP recovers much of this from the static latent
($0.89$ versus
$0.95$), so the RSSM mainly makes cyclic information more linearly accessible.
Return-to-go is near zero for both models, and time-to-fall is unreliable due to
censoring (Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}).

\subsection{Generative quality and probe quality decouple}
\label{sec:gen}
At matched training (Table~\ref{tab:nll}), the RSSM reconstructs the current
frame with lower NLL than the VAE ($0.62$--$0.67$ versus $1.77$--$1.95$ for the
two $500$K seeds, and lower per-frame NLL on $90$--$97\%$ of frames), yet its
current-state probe \rtwo{} is only marginally higher. Seed~0, trained longer,
has still lower NLL ($0.36$) but probe \rtwo{} within seed noise. Reconstruction
likelihood and linear-probe decodability therefore agree only partially.

\section{Discussion}

In this near-fully observed proprioceptive Walker setting, the sequential latent
does not encode more of the current observable state than a static VAE with a
hand-specified causal summary of past latents. Its advantages are predictive and
coordinate-shaping: the action-conditioned prior forecasts better than a
posterior readout under both probe families, and under the primary linear probe
it also beats the VAE-dynamics baseline in all seeds ($0.50$ versus $0.46$ at
$20$ steps), although the MLP comparison ties at that horizon. The RSSM also
makes gait phase linearly decodable, while a nonlinear probe recovers most of it
from the static latent. Separately, lower reconstruction NLL does not yield a
matching gain in linear state decodability, so likelihood and probe \rtwo{}
should not be treated as interchangeable measures of representation quality.
Practically, when only current-state decoding is needed, a static VAE with simple
causal pooling is enough; sequential pretraining is most useful for multi-step
prediction and for making cyclic structure linearly accessible.

\textbf{Limitations.} The comparison still differs in latent family, prior,
objective, and probed dimensionality ($640$ versus $128$); matched-width probes
control only part of this. The VAE-dynamics baseline is a post-hoc model trained
on frozen VAE latents from probe episodes, whereas the RSSM transition was
co-trained with its representation; stronger VAE dynamics could narrow the
long-horizon gap. We report pooled, variance-weighted $R^2$, leaving
per-component probes to future work. Finally, we study one domain, one model
size, and proprioception, where a single frame nearly determines current state.
Pixel and other partially observed regimes remain untested, and only one seed
ran to $1.1$M steps, so the training-length effect on NLL is suggestive.

\section{Null and unreliable targets}
\label{app:nulls}
Two derived targets are reported for completeness but excluded from the main
findings. Return-to-go (discounted future reward) has near-zero \rtwo{} for both
models ($\approx-0.01$ posterior, $\approx0$ VAE) on Walker-Walk, where per-step
reward is nearly constant and not linearly decodable. Time-to-fall is highly
seed-dependent (posterior \rtwo{} of $0.06$, $0.50$, and $0.98$ across seeds)
because a competent walker rarely falls, so the target is dominated by rare,
heavily right-censored events; we therefore do not draw conclusions from it.

\section{Reproducibility details}
DreamerV3 uses the \texttt{dmc\_proprio}/\texttt{size1m} configuration
($h_t\in\mathbb{R}^{512}$, $z_t$ a set of $32{\times}4$ categoricals,
encoder and decoder MLPs of width $64$, depth $3$, symlog preprocessing). The
VAE uses the same encoder and decoder architecture, with a $128$-dimensional Gaussian
latent, $\beta{=}1$, trained from scratch for $60$K steps (Adam, lr
$3\times10^{-4}$) on each seed's replay buffer. Probes use ridge
(regularization selected on a validation split) and a two-hidden-layer,
width-$256$ ReLU MLP. The VAE-latent dynamics baseline is a two-hidden-layer
width-$256$ residual MLP trained with a curriculum multi-step rollout loss (the
rollout length ramped $1\to20$) for $6000$ steps (Adam, lr $10^{-3}$, gradient
clip $1.0$) on the probe train episodes. The probe set is $40$ held-out episodes
of $1000$ steps from the frozen evaluation policy with logged physics state. All
figures are regenerated by
\texttt{result\_images/build\_summary\_figures.py} from the per-seed outputs.
